# Final CV Quality Check

Run this reference after the CV content and formatting are complete. Treat the exported PDF—not only the editor view—as the source of truth.

## 1. Verify the two-page limit

1. Export the final CV to PDF.
2. Confirm that the PDF has one or two pages only.
3. Check both pages visually for clipping, page-break errors, orphan headings, broken indentation, and date alignment.

If the CV exceeds two pages, use this reduction order:

```text
Remove low-relevance experience or obsolete detail
→ merge overlapping bullets
→ shorten context while retaining action + result
→ move secondary tools to a compact skills line
→ tighten vertical spacing modestly
→ reduce font size only within readable limits
```

Do not compress the document by deleting results, shrinking text below roughly 9.5 pt, or using crowded margins. A shorter, more selective CV is stronger than an unreadable two-page CV.

## 2. Make the language professional and human

Use clear, familiar professional vocabulary. Let demonstrated work carry the tone; do not attempt to sound sophisticated through rare words, inflated claims, or generic enthusiasm.

Prefer:

```text
coordinated, analyzed, built, created, improved, managed, negotiated,
planned, produced, supported, tested, wrote
```

Use with caution or replace when they do not add precision:

```text
leverage, spearhead, synergize, utilize, delve, navigate, foster,
passionate, dynamic, results-driven, game-changing, seamless, robust
```

Rewrite toward evidence:

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| “Spearheaded a robust strategy to leverage digital channels.” | “**Digital channel strategy:** Planned content across [channels] and reviewed engagement data to refine targeting.” |
| “Passionate team player with excellent communication skills.” | “**Cross-team coordination:** Aligned [teams/stakeholders] on [deliverable], keeping [workstream] on schedule.” |
| “Utilized SQL to derive actionable insights.” | “**Data analysis:** Queried [dataset] in SQL to identify [pattern] and inform [decision].” |

Use the JD’s terminology only when it names a demonstrated responsibility, deliverable, tool, or stakeholder. Natural alignment is more credible than keyword density.

## 3. Remove repetitive phrasing

Scan for the same lead verb, summary phrase, sentence pattern, or claim appearing three or more times in a nearby section. Keep essential repeated keywords if the JD requires them, but vary the surrounding language and evidence.

Apply these paraphrase moves without changing facts:

- Change the analytical angle: planning → execution → stakeholder coordination → measurement → improvement.
- Swap a repeated generic verb for a precise action: “managed” → “scheduled,” “negotiated,” “reviewed,” “delivered,” or “coordinated,” as supported by evidence.
- Lead one bullet with scope and another with outcome, while retaining the phrase + colon structure.
- Merge duplicate bullets that prove the same capability and keep the stronger result.

Do not paraphrase a key JD term away merely to avoid repetition. Keep it once in a high-value bullet, then demonstrate the same capability through specific actions elsewhere.

## 4. Final recruiter scan

Read the first half-page in 15 seconds. Confirm that it reveals:

```text
Target role or professional direction
→ strongest relevant capability
→ evidence of scope/impact
→ reason to keep reading
```

Then search the document for:

- empty adjectives without evidence;
- unsupported percentages or scale claims;
- mismatched verb tense;
- sentence fragments that obscure agency;
- duplicate skills in the summary, roles, and skills section;
- more than two pages in the exported PDF.
