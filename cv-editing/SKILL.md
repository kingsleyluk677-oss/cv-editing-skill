# CV Editing

## Aim

Create a truthful, skimmable CV that makes the candidate’s most relevant evidence easy to find. Treat the JD as a relevance and language map—not a script for inventing accomplishments.

Use the compact single-column layout in [references/layout.md](references/layout.md) when creating or reformatting a CV. It derives only generic layout rules from the supplied template; do not reuse its personal content.

Use [references/quality-check.md](references/quality-check.md) for the final two-page, professional-language, repetition, and JD-alignment pass.

## Three-phase workflow

### Phase 1: collect evidence and establish fit

Start with the JD and an existing CV when both are available. Extract the JD signals, map demonstrated experience to them, and identify evidence gaps before drafting.

If no CV is available, or the CV does not contain enough evidence to tailor safely, conduct a short, structured interview before generating the CV. Request only job-relevant information. Ask for:

1. The target role, seniority, location/work authorization requirements if relevant, and the most important JD outcomes.
2. The candidate’s most relevant roles, projects, education, certifications, tools, languages, and domain knowledge.
3. Two or three concrete achievement stories: context/goal, personal action, scope, and result.
4. Evidence of collaboration, leadership, client/stakeholder work, analysis, or delivery that matches the JD.
5. Verified metrics, portfolio links, and constraints such as page limit or employment dates.

Do not request identity documents, passwords, unrelated personal details, or unverified claims. Summarize the resulting evidence map and flag only the gaps that materially affect CV accuracy.

### Phase 2: draft, format, and check

Generate the CV from the confirmed evidence. Prioritize roles and bullets by JD relevance, write STAR-based bullets in the required summary phrase + colon format, apply the layout system, and run the pre-delivery quality pass. Export to PDF and verify the two-page limit before presenting a final version.

### Phase 3: iterate on user feedback

Treat each user comment as a scoped revision request. Adjust content, wording, order, or layout without introducing unsupported claims. Re-run the affected JD, language, repetition, and page-count checks after each substantive revision. Briefly state what changed and retain the candidate’s factual record.

## Intake and JD mapping

Before rewriting, collect the current CV, target JD, role seniority, and any relevant constraints (page limit, country, application system, portfolio/links). If the CV is absent or incomplete, run the Phase 1 interview. If a JD is absent, retain a broadly relevant, reverse-chronological CV and mark tailoring opportunities rather than guessing.

Extract from the JD:

1. **Role outcomes:** What must the hire deliver or improve?
2. **Core responsibilities:** Which recurring actions matter most?
3. **Required and preferred capabilities:** Skills, tools, domains, stakeholders, languages, certifications, and work conditions.
4. **Evidence signals:** Scale, metrics, ownership level, leadership, collaboration, client-facing work, or analytical rigor.
5. **Keywords:** Use the employer’s accurate terminology where it matches the candidate’s demonstrated work.

Map every experience to those signals using the candidate’s actual evidence. Then prioritize:

```text
Directly relevant, high-impact experience
→ directly relevant supporting experience
→ transferable experience with clear evidence
→ brief additional experience
```

Reorder bullets within a role by JD relevance and impact. When the application permits, place the most relevant roles in a “Relevant Experience” section before “Additional Experience”; otherwise retain the requested/reverse-chronological structure and foreground relevance through bullet order and wording. Never alter dates, job titles, employers, scope, metrics, or tools to improve a match.

## Write STAR evidence bullets

Use STAR as the underlying evidence model:

```text
Situation/Task: context, challenge, responsibility, or target.
Action: specific action personally taken.
Result: measurable outcome, stakeholder benefit, decision enabled, or concrete deliverable.
```

Compress STAR for a CV. Do not force all four labels into every bullet. Include only the context needed to make the action and result credible, and lead with the action/result most relevant to the JD.

Write every experience bullet in this required form:

```text
• **[2–5 word summary phrase]:** [action + relevant context + result/evidence].
```

Make the phrase a concise, job-relevant label—not a vague adjective. Use an active verb after the colon and include a credible result whenever available.

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| “Responsible for social media.” | “**Multi-channel campaign delivery:** Planned and published content across [channels], adapting messages by audience and tracking engagement to refine the next campaign.” |
| “Helped with event planning.” | “**Stakeholder event coordination:** Coordinated [vendors/teams], schedules, and event materials for [event], delivering [scope/result].” |
| “Used SQL for analysis.” | “**Audience insight analysis:** Queried [dataset] in SQL to identify [pattern], supplying evidence for [decision/campaign/product change].” |

Use numbers only when supplied or verifiable. When no numerical result exists, state a concrete outcome: a decision enabled, process improved, asset delivered, audience reached, partnership secured, risk reduced, or deadline met. Attribute team results accurately with verbs such as “contributed to,” “coordinated,” “co-led,” or “supported” when appropriate.

## Tailor language without keyword stuffing

Mirror the JD’s language only where it truthfully describes the candidate’s work. Translate a transferable experience into the employer’s vocabulary by preserving the underlying action and result.

```text
JD: “Manage cross-functional campaign delivery.”
Evidence: “**Cross-functional campaign delivery:** Coordinated [teams] to launch [campaign], aligning timelines, creative assets, and reporting.”
```

Avoid a generic skills list as a substitute for evidence. Place a skill in the skills section only when it is relevant and demonstrated elsewhere, or label it accurately by proficiency/context.

Use this test for every bullet:

```text
Can a recruiter see the JD-relevant capability?
Can they see the candidate’s individual action?
Can they see evidence of scope, quality, or result?
```

If one answer is no, revise the bullet or ask for the missing fact. Do not fill the gap with inflated language.

## Structure the CV

Use the following default order; adjust only when the JD or candidate profile makes a different order more persuasive:

1. Header and professional contact details
2. Education, if recent, required, or an especially strong role signal
3. Targeted professional summary and core competencies, when they add information beyond the experience section
4. Relevant professional experience
5. Additional experience, leadership, projects, or selected portfolio
6. Skills, languages, tools, and certifications

For each role, use this hierarchy:

```text
Job title (bold, left)                                         Dates (bold, right)
Employer, location (italic, left)
• Summary phrase: STAR evidence.
• Summary phrase: STAR evidence.
```

Keep roles and dates factual and consistent. Keep the candidate’s strongest, most JD-relevant evidence near the top of the page and near the start of each role.

## Format for fast reading

Apply [references/layout.md](references/layout.md) for the visual system. Preserve these rules in any format:

- Use a single column and a consistent left edge for scanability and ATS compatibility.
- Use section bands and typography—not decorative graphics—to establish hierarchy.
- Align dates consistently on the right; keep employer/location subordinate to job title.
- Use a restrained accent color, high-contrast text, and ample but economical whitespace.
- Keep bullets compact, normally one to two lines; split a dense bullet instead of shrinking text excessively.
- Use consistent punctuation and date style. Use page numbers on multi-page CVs.
- Include portfolio links or QR codes only when relevant and functioning; never let them displace essential experience evidence.

## Run the pre-delivery quality pass

Perform this pass after editing content and before delivery. Fix substantive problems before cosmetic ones.

1. **Two-page limit.** Render or export the final document to PDF and confirm that it has no more than two pages. If it exceeds two pages, remove low-relevance content, consolidate bullets, and tighten spacing before reducing font size. Do not use unreadably small type to force a fit.
2. **Professional language.** Replace vague, inflated, overly ornate, or AI-sounding wording with precise common professional language. Prefer a concrete action, scope, and result over generic claims such as “results-driven,” “passionate,” “leveraged,” “delved into,” or “spearheaded” when plainer truthful wording is available.
3. **JD alignment.** Confirm that the wording naturally reflects the target JD’s responsibilities and terminology only where the CV supplies evidence. Do not keyword-stuff or copy the JD verbatim.
4. **Repetition.** Scan summaries and bullets for repeated sentence openings, duplicated skill claims, and recycled phrasing. Paraphrase repetitive items while preserving the factual claim and the lead-in phrase + colon format.
5. **Layout integrity.** Confirm that no heading is stranded at the bottom of a page, dates remain aligned, bullets do not wrap awkwardly, and the hierarchy still scans in seconds.

Use the detailed rubrics and rewrite patterns in [references/quality-check.md](references/quality-check.md).

## Final quality check

Before delivering a CV, confirm:

- Every bullet begins with a short summary phrase followed by a colon.
- Every experience bullet contains at least an action and evidence/result, with context where needed.
- The first bullets and roles answer the JD’s most important requirements.
- Keywords are accurate, natural, and supported by evidence.
- The document contains no fabricated metrics, responsibilities, credentials, or tools.
- Titles, employers, dates, punctuation, alignment, and typographic hierarchy are consistent.
- The first half-page tells a recruiter what role the candidate fits and why.
- The exported PDF is two pages or fewer, uses professional natural language, has no avoidable repetition, and keeps JD wording evidence-based.
